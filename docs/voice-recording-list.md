# Voice recording list (your voice)

These recordings replace the phone's robot voice where it is weakest: single
sounds, the pronunciation-tip words, and the everyday sentences beginners hear
most. **111 short clips in total; about 1½–2 hours including retakes.**

## How to record

- **Where:** a quiet room with soft things around (a bedroom with curtains and a
  bed is better than an empty hall). Turn off fans and AC.
- **Phone:** any recent phone's voice recorder is fine. Hold it 20–30 cm from
  your mouth, slightly to the side (not straight in front, to avoid "p" puffs).
- **Format:** the recorder's default (`.m4a` or `.mp3`) is fine; I convert them.
- **Each clip:** stay silent for about 1 second, say the item **once, clearly, at a normal teaching speed**, then stay silent for 1 more second.
  Don't read the file name aloud.
- **Natural Indian English is perfect.** The goal is clear and understandable,
  not a British or American accent.
- **File name:** use the name in the first column (e.g. `snd-th-voiceless.m4a`).
  If renaming is hard, record in the order below and send them numbered; I can
  match them.
- Record a short test first and send it to me; I'll check volume and noise
  before you record everything.

## Optional — slow versions (later)

The app already has a "हळू" (slow) button that slows down audio; recorded
clips can be slowed the same way, so separate slow recordings are not needed.

## Sending the files

Put them all in one folder (e.g. `~/Desktop/boluya-voice/`) and tell Claude
the folder path. **Don't put them in the project folder yourself.** Claude
converts, trims silence, normalises volume and adds them to the app, with the
phone voice kept as a fallback.

By recording, you agree that your voice is used in the English Boluya app.
If someone else records, get their written permission first.
